**Where you stand**  
There is **no legal limit** on the size of a security deposit for residential rentals in Karnataka. The Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 does not prescribe a maximum deposit, and the Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act, 2025 did not add any such cap [1]. Therefore a “two‑month deposit cap” is not found in the current statutes. The amount you must pay and when it must be returned is whatever your rental agreement specifies [1].

**What you can do**  
1. **Check your written rental agreement** – it governs the deposit amount and the conditions for its return.  
2. **Ask the landlord in writing** (email or message) to confirm the deposit amount agreed and request a receipt [4].  
3. If the landlord refuses to return excess deposit that the agreement says is refundable, you can:  
   - Send a formal legal notice stating the facts, the amount owed, and a deadline [4];  
   - Seek settlement through a Lok Adalat via the District Legal Services Authority (cheapest route) [4];  
   - File a civil suit for recovery of the deposit as a breach of contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 [4].

**Next step**  
Write a short email to your landlord quoting the deposit amount stated in your rental agreement and ask for the full amount to be returned by a reasonable date (e.g., within 15 days). Keep a copy for your records. If you receive no satisfactory reply, the next step would be to send a formal legal notice. **Where you stand**  
If your landlord keeps the security deposit that your rental agreement says must be returned, it is a breach of contract and you may claim compensation for the loss caused [1]. The law does not specify a statutory right to interest on a delayed refund; the passages contain no Supreme Court judgment on that point.  

**What you can do**  
1. Send a written demand (message or email) asking the landlord to return the deposit by a clear date and keep any reply [4].  
2. If there is no payment, send a legal notice by registered post stating the amount, deadline and that you will take legal action [4].  
3. If the landlord agrees, you can try settlement through a Lok Adalat under the District Legal Services Authority; any award there is final and binding [4].  
4. If the above steps fail, you may file a civil suit for breach of contract. Such a suit must generally be filed within three years from the date of breach [4].  

**Next step**  
Draft and send a written demand to your landlord asking for the deposit back within, for example, 7 days, and keep a copy of the communication [4].
