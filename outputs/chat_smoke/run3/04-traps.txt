**Where you stand**  
The Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 does not set any maximum amount for a security deposit; the law only talks about a tenant depositing rent with the Controller when the landlord refuses it [1]. The 2025 amendment also did not add a deposit cap [1]. Therefore, a “two-month deposit cap” is not found in the current Karnataka rent statutes [1].

**What you can do**  
1. Check whether the Karnataka Rent Act actually applies to your home (it applies only to low-rent, older premises in certain municipal areas) [2][4].

You: which supreme court judgment says landlords must pay interest on late deposit refunds?

LawBuddy [advice]:
**Where you stand**  
The law on security-deposit refunds in Karnataka is mainly governed by the rental agreement and, where the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 applies, by its contract provisions.  The Act does not contain any rule about a landlord having to pay interest for a delayed refund, and my sources do not mention any Supreme Court judgment on that point [1][3].

**What you can do**  
1. Ask the landlord in writing to return the deposit, giving a clear deadline.  
2. If the landlord does not comply, send a formal legal notice stating the amount owed and a final date for payment.  
3. If the dispute remains unresolved, you can seek settlement through a Lok Adalat via the District Legal Services Authority, which is the cheapest and quickest formal route.  
4. As a last resort, you may file a civil suit for breach of contract to recover the deposit (and any other compensation the court may award) [1].

**Next step**  
Send a written request (email or message) to the landlord asking for the deposit back and specifying a reasonable date for payment (for example, within 7 days). Keep any proof of the deposit payment (bank slip, UPI receipt, cash receipt) if you have it, as it will be needed later. If the landlord does not respond or refuses, you can move to the next step of issuing a legal notice.
