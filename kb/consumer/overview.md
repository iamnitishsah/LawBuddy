If you paid for goods or a service and got something defective, deficient, not as promised, or no refund that was
due, the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 protects you. It applies to purchases made online or offline, including by
teleshopping and direct selling (Consumer Protection Act, 2019, s. 2(7), Explanation (b)).

You can complain about (Consumer Protection Act, 2019, s. 2(6)):
- goods with a defect: a fault or shortcoming in quality, quantity, purity or standard (s. 2(10));
- a service with a deficiency: a shortcoming in quality or manner of performance, including negligence or
  deliberately withholding relevant information (s. 2(11));
- an unfair trade practice, or being charged more than the displayed, fixed or agreed price.

If the complaint is proved, a Consumer Commission can order the trader to remove the defect, replace the goods,
return the price with interest, pay compensation for loss or injury, pay punitive damages, and pay your costs
(Consumer Protection Act, 2019, s. 39(1)).

Usual path: complain to the seller (and the platform's grievance officer) → National Consumer Helpline 1915 →
complaint to the District Consumer Commission, which can be filed online on e-Jagriti.
