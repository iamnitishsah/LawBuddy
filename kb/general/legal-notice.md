A legal notice is a formal letter telling the other side what they owe or must do, by when, and that you will take
legal action if they do not. For deposit, refund and salary disputes, none of the laws in LawBuddy's sources requires
one before going to a Consumer Commission, a wage authority or a Lok Adalat, but it is a good first formal step
because:

- it often makes the other side pay or settle, at little cost;
- it creates a dated record that you demanded payment, which helps prove your case and when the dispute began;
- it forces you to set out the facts and the amount clearly.

A good notice states: who you are and who it is addressed to; the facts in date order; the amount or action
demanded; a deadline (commonly 15 days); and what you will do if they do not comply. It should be factual and
polite, with no threats beyond lawful action, and no facts you cannot prove.

Send it by registered post or speed post with acknowledgement, and also by email or WhatsApp. Keep the postal receipt
and tracking record. A notice can be sent by you yourself or through a lawyer; one sent through a lawyer often
carries more weight.
