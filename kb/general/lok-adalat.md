Lok Adalats are organised by the State and District Legal Services Authorities (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987,
s. 19). They settle disputes by compromise.

- **Before a case is filed:** one party can apply to the Legal Services Authority to have a dispute that is not yet in
  court referred to a Lok Adalat (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, s. 20(2)). The other side is heard before
  the matter is referred.
- **Result:** if both sides settle, the award is treated as a decree of a civil court, is final and binding on all
  parties, and no appeal lies against it. If a pending court case is settled this way, the court fee paid is
  refunded (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, s. 21).
- **If there is no settlement,** the parties are advised to seek their remedy in court (s. 20(6)).

Lok Adalat suits disputes where the other side may be willing to pay something to close the matter, such as a
deposit or dues dispute. Contact your District Legal Services Authority to ask about the next Lok Adalat.
