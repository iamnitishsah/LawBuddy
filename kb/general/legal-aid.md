The Constitution directs the State to provide free legal aid so that no citizen is denied justice because of
economic or other disabilities (Constitution of India, Article 39A). The Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 puts
this into practice.

You are entitled to free legal services to file or defend a case if you are (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987,
s. 12):
- a woman or a child;
- a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe;
- a person with a disability;
- an industrial workman;
- a victim of trafficking, or of a mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake or
  industrial disaster;
- in custody; or
- a person whose annual income is below the limit set by the State Government (for cases in courts other than the
  Supreme Court).

The Authority must also be satisfied that you have a prima facie case (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987,
s. 13(1)).

**Where to ask:** the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) at your district court, the Taluk Legal Services
Committee, the Karnataka State Legal Services Authority (kslsa.kar.nic.in), or the NALSA helpline **15100**
(toll-free). Ask them for the current income limit in Karnataka.
