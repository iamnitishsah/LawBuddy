A security deposit is money you gave the landlord to hold, to be returned when you leave. Whether you get it back,
and how much may be deducted, is decided mainly by your rental agreement.

For most rented homes in Karnataka today, a dispute over a deposit is a dispute about a contract. The Karnataka
Rent Act, 1999 does not apply to many homes: it excludes premises whose rent is above a low limit and buildings
constructed or substantially renovated in the last fifteen years (Karnataka Rent Act, 1999, s. 2(3)). When the
landlord keeps money the agreement says must be returned, that is a breach of contract, and the person who suffers
from a breach can claim compensation for the loss it caused (Indian Contract Act, 1872, s. 73).

The usual path, from cheapest to most formal:
1. Ask in writing (message or email) for the refund, with a date to pay by.
2. Send a legal notice that sets out the facts, the amount and a deadline.
3. Try settlement through a Lok Adalat via the District Legal Services Authority.
4. File a civil suit to recover the money.
