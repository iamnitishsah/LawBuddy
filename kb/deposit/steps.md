1. **Written demand.** Message or email the landlord asking for the deposit by a clear date (for example, 7 days).
   Keep the reply, or the lack of one.
2. **Legal notice.** If there is no payment, send a legal notice by registered post (and email or WhatsApp) stating
   the facts, the amount due, the deadline and that you will take legal action. LawBuddy can draft one for you to
   review and sign.
3. **Lok Adalat (settlement).** The District Legal Services Authority can take up a dispute that is not yet in court
   on the application of one party and refer it to a Lok Adalat (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, s. 20). A
   settlement there becomes an award that is final and binding, with no appeal (Legal Services Authorities Act,
   1987, s. 21). It only works if the landlord agrees to settle.
4. **Civil suit.** If nothing else works, file a suit to recover the money. Which court hears it depends on the
   amount and the place, so ask a lawyer or your DLSA before filing.

Time limit: a suit for compensation for breach of a contract must generally be filed within three years from when
the contract is broken (Limitation Act, 1963, Schedule, Article 55). Act early; the exact start date depends on
your facts.
