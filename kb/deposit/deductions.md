What can be deducted depends on your rental agreement. Read its clauses on the deposit, notice period, painting,
repairs and unpaid bills.

Under contract law, a party who breaks a contract must compensate only the loss that naturally arises from that
breach; remote or indirect losses are not compensated (Indian Contract Act, 1872, s. 73). So a landlord can
generally deduct, for example, unpaid rent or the cost of real damage that you caused, but should be able to show
what the loss was. If the agreement names a fixed amount payable for a breach (for example, a penalty for leaving
without notice), the landlord can claim only reasonable compensation up to that amount, not automatically the whole
of it (Indian Contract Act, 1872, s. 74).

Practical steps:
- Ask the landlord for an itemised list of deductions with bills or photos.
- Compare each deduction with the agreement.
- Pay or accept only what the agreement and the evidence support, and dispute the rest in writing.
